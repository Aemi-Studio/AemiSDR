# Diary — 2026-10-20 (13)

## Clear glass, from "no better than nothing" to 92.8

Glass Lab compares the package against native `clear` Liquid Glass on one booted simulator, over 8 shapes (capsule, circle,
square, rounded rectangles of radius 8, 16, 32 and 60, a triangle) and 4 backgrounds, similarity being SSIM x 100 inside the
shape's box. The captures were wrong at first: the app had not drawn when the screenshot was taken, so the "nothing" baseline
and the whole `complex` column read 100 on blank screens. Fixed by building the lab in Release and waiting; two launches of
the same scene are now byte-identical.

### What native clear glass is, by measurement

| Claim | Probe | Verdict |

### Results (similarity x100, mean over 8 shapes x 4 backgrounds)

old lens about 30, nothing about 52 | clear glass, single Gaussian 89.6 | with the ring 89.6 | **two-Gaussian blur 92.8 (min 88.1)**.
Interior SSIM is now about 98.8 (99.8 on the complex pattern): the interior is essentially solved. The remaining error is at the
edges: by distance from the edge, the rim 3 to 15 px in carries 41 to 55% of the loss, 15 to 40 px another 17 to 21%, the
2 px ring 10 to 30%, the interior only 2 to 13%.

### Things that went wrong on the way

- A test helper edit silently missed because the formatter had reflowed the lines; the ring test then read a 48-pixel-wide
  target with a 40-pixel stride and showed a sheared image. Found by dumping the interpolated position from the shader, not
  by guessing at the shader.
- An unsigned peak finder on the checker gave a nonsense displacement map (negative sources). Signed, alternating edges fixed
  the far field; near the edge the mapping folds (the displacement changes faster than one source pixel per screen pixel), so
  one-edge-per-edge counting cannot follow it. Free fitting there falls into local minima for the same reason.
- My mutation test of the shape gradient first passed because the test compared only distances.

### The rim band, and what did not help

The physical bevel profile `t * tan(theta - asin(sin theta / n))`, refitted with the correct blur and the flat-calibrated tone held
fixed so sigma could not drift, is worse again (rim-band rms 0.056 and 0.058 against 0.045 for the Gaussian). A free-form
profile with 15 knots gains 18% and is the Gaussian within noise. Walking the checker's edges to read the sampling map directly
confirms the far field (edges every 16 px, displacement 0 beyond about 60 px) and shows why the near edge is hard: the
displacement changes faster than one source pixel per screen pixel, so the mapping folds and counting edges cannot follow it.
The Gaussian stays. The rim band 3 to 15 px in remains the largest source of error and is not solved.

### Performance (step 5), measured the same way as before (rotating order, a control)

GPU per frame at 3x, 2048 x 2048 incompressible source: typical glass (300 x 200 pt) about 0.41 to 0.48 ms, a large 650 pt square
about 2.9 ms. The blur dominates, so the kernel's taps were paired: two neighbouring taps are one fetch at the weighted
mean offset, the bilinear hardware applying both weights (the zeroth and first moments are preserved exactly, tested).
Plain one-fetch-per-texel costs x1.30 to x1.48 of paired across three runs against a control of x0.98 to x1.01: about 25 to 32%
saved, output unchanged within the exact-Gaussian test's 1.5/255. Not yet tried: computing the wide halo at half resolution.
