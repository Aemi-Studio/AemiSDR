## Capture ownership: reproduced, then corrected

New production-path tests on iOS 18.6 failed with seven issues before the correction:
- 257×257 and 258×257 requests returned the same pooled bridge, despite render sizing that ring exactly.
- Unregistering a consumer let another consumer overwrite an outstanding texture's slot.
- Unknown and already-unregistered consumer IDs could render.
- A zero-sized LiquidLensUIView invoked its release callback immediately and kept that texture for later layout retries.

Root causes: bucketed pool keys disagreed with exact-size allocation; unregister erased reservations before completion;
the view released the source after its first submission or an early exit despite retaining it for future renders.

The pool now keys on exact dimensions. A retired consumer cannot render again, but its reservations remain until completion.
A TextureSourceLease owns the current source until replacement or view destruction. Every GPU submission owns an
independent TextureReadLease; completion drops that hold idempotently, outside its lock. The source callback runs only
after both the view's hold and every GPU read are gone. Existing renderer early-failure callbacks also finish the read.

Completion closures retain the pixel-buffer, CVMetalTexture and Metal-texture wrappers across resize or bridge destruction.
This makes the backing lifetime explicit; a hardware use-after-free was not reproduced and is not claimed.

Six tests cover exact-size pooling, consumer retirement, rejected consumers, delayed draw retry, multiple GPU holds and
old-generation completions after resize. The complete iOS 18.6 suite passes: 113 tests in 21 suites.
Additional sustained GPU-load/resize profiling and device checks remain, rather than claiming all lifetime risks exhausted.
